Why I Built Kashmir Stay and MakeMe Top — The Full Story by Abrar Ali (Abrarr Tallks)

Web DesignAbrar Ali·27 May 2026·13 min read

Everything Started with a Phone Call

My name is Abrar Ali — web designer, developer, SEO expert, and founder of rebrar.in. Online, most people know me as Abrarr Tallks. I am from Srinagar, Jammu & Kashmir.

In 2025, I got a phone call from a houseboat owner on Dal Lake. He had heard I build websites for travel businesses. He wanted to know how much it would cost to get his houseboat found on Google.

I asked him the standard questions: How long have you been operating? How many boats do you have? What is the condition — budget, mid-range, luxury? Do you have photos?

His answers: 28 years operating. Three boats. Mid-range with one premium boat. Good photos on his phone but never uploaded anywhere.

Twenty-eight years. A family business on Dal Lake that had survived floods, political turbulence, and a global pandemic. Two boats. Good product. Zero digital presence. Zero Google footprint. Zero way for the millions of tourists searching "houseboat Dal Lake" every month to find him.

I built him a website. It ranked. He started getting enquiries. He was genuinely shocked that a tourist from Hyderabad had found him on Google.

But as I drove home from that meeting, something was bothering me. The website I had just built cost him ₹15,000. The SEO would take 3–6 months to produce results. And he was just one of hundreds — maybe thousands — of J&K travel businesses in exactly the same position. Invisible. Not because they lacked quality. Because they lacked digital infrastructure.

I could not build individual websites for all of them. But I could build something that served all of them at once.

That thought was the seed of everything that came next.

The Problem I Was Watching Get Worse Every Year

Kashmir receives millions of tourists every year. Tourism is the primary economic engine of the valley. It supports houseboat families, resort owners, tour operators, local guides, shikara operators, craft sellers, taxi drivers — a complex ecosystem of people whose livelihoods depend on tourist spending.

But here is what I was watching happen in that ecosystem:

The digital side of Kashmir tourism was being captured entirely by large national platforms — MakeMyTrip, Booking.com, Thrillophilia, and similar OTAs. When a tourist searched "Kashmir travel package" or "houseboat Srinagar" on Google, the first page of results was dominated by these platforms.

The local businesses? They were the product — but they were not in control of their own digital presence. They depended on the OTAs to list them, and the OTAs charged 15–25% commission on every booking that came through. For a small houseboat operation already running on thin margins, losing a quarter of every booking to a platform was devastating.

And the businesses that could not afford even to list on OTAs — the smallest operators, the newest businesses, the ones in less-discovered J&K destinations — were completely absent from the digital economy of Kashmir tourism. They did not exist online. Tourists could not find them. Their potential market was invisible to them and they were invisible to it.

This was not a small problem. This was structural. And it was getting worse as more tourist decision-making moved online.

Why I Did Not Just Build More Websites

The obvious response to this problem — for someone who builds websites — is to build more websites. That is what I had been doing. And it worked for individual clients. But individual websites do not solve a structural problem.

A small houseboat owner who pays ₹15,000 for a website still needs to wait 3–6 months for SEO to kick in. They still need to pay for annual hosting and domain renewal. They still need someone to update their content. And they still start with zero domain authority — competing from scratch against OTAs that have been building authority for 15 years.

A directory solves this differently. A single platform with high domain authority, consistent SEO architecture, and a curated collection of verified J&K travel businesses creates collective visibility that no individual website could achieve alone. When a tourist searches "tour operator Pahalgam" and finds a verified directory page, every business on that page benefits from the directory's authority — not just the one that could afford the best individual website.

That is the core logic behind MakeMe Top Business Profile.

Building MakeMe Top Business Profile (MBP)

The Decision to Build

I made the decision to build MBP in late 2024. The name came first: MakeMe Top — make my business appear at the top of Google. Every local business owner I had spoken to had the same goal. The name states it directly.

I knew what the platform needed to do:

  • Let any J&K travel business create a verified listing without technical knowledge
  • Index every listing on Google with proper Schema.org structured data within 48 hours
  • Build trust with tourists through a Govt Verified badge for registered businesses
  • Connect tourists directly with businesses — no commission, no booking layer
  • Price the listing so small businesses could afford it — ₹799/month maximum

The Technical Build

I built MBP entirely myself — front-end, back-end, database, admin panel, payment integration, and SEO architecture.

The tech stack: HTML, CSS, vanilla JavaScript, and Firebase (Firestore for the listing database, Authentication for business owner logins, Storage for photos). Payment processing through Razorpay. Hosting on a custom domain with full HTTPS.

The SEO architecture was the most important technical decision. Every listing page is generated with:

  • A unique URL based on the business name and location — /listing/houseboat-dal-lake-srinagar/
  • Full Schema.org LocalBusiness structured data — Google reads the business name, type, location, phone, description precisely
  • XML sitemap entry — submitted to Google Search Console immediately upon approval
  • Unique meta title and description — keyword-targeted for the specific business type and location
  • Geo tags — IN-JK, Srinagar coordinates, so Google understands this is location-specific content

The result: approved listings appear in Google search results within 48 hours. A houseboat owner with no technical knowledge, no existing website, and no previous Google presence becomes Google-visible within two days of paying ₹799.

The Govt Verified Badge — Why It Matters

One early decision that proved to be critically important: the Govt Verified badge.

I noticed that tourist queries about Kashmir businesses often included trust signals — "registered tour operator Kashmir", "government approved houseboat Srinagar". Tourists — especially those visiting Kashmir for the first time — were worried about legitimacy.

The Govt Verified badge gives businesses that have submitted a government registration document a visible trust signal on their listing. It does not guarantee anything — MBP is transparent about that. But it communicates that the business has taken the step of formal registration and that MBP has received documentation to that effect.

Listings with the Govt Verified badge receive significantly more tourist enquiries than unverified listings. This creates a natural incentive for business owners to formalise their registration — which is good for the broader tourism ecosystem.

The Problem MBP Created — One Platform, Two Audiences

As MBP grew, a tension emerged that I had not fully anticipated.

MBP was attracting two completely different types of users: business owners who wanted to list their businesses, and tourists who wanted to find verified travel businesses in Kashmir.

These two audiences have completely different needs, different mental models, and different journeys through any platform. A business owner landing on MBP needs a professional-feeling listing management tool — a dashboard, pricing information, badge verification upload, analytics. A tourist landing on MBP needs a beautiful, fast, image-led discovery experience — filters by location and type, photos, AI-powered recommendations, destination information.

A single platform trying to serve both audiences ends up doing neither well. The homepage has to split its attention. The navigation has to work for two fundamentally different user flows. The tone of the copywriting has to speak to a business owner and a tourist simultaneously — and those are very different conversations.

I spent several weeks thinking about this problem. The solution was obvious once I framed it clearly:

Build two separate platforms. One for each audience. Powered by the same verified listing database.

Building Kashmir Stay (kashmirstay.in)

The Name

The name Kashmir Stay took longer to decide than anything technical. I went through dozens of options. Eventually, the simplicity of Kashmir Stay became obvious: it describes exactly what a tourist is looking for. Not accommodation specifically. The full act of staying in Kashmir — living it, experiencing it, being inside it. kashmirstay.in was available and it was perfect.

What Kashmir Stay Had to Be

I built Kashmir Stay with one question guiding every decision: what does a tourist actually need when they are planning a Kashmir trip and looking for a place to stay or a tour operator?

The answers from watching how tourists interact with travel platforms online:

  • They want beautiful, high-quality images — they are buying a dream, not a database entry
  • They want to filter quickly by location, type and budget — they know what part of Kashmir they want to visit
  • They want trust signals — verification, reviews, registration documents
  • They want to contact the business directly — not go through a booking platform that takes commission
  • They want destination information — what is Sonamarg like in October? When does Gulmarg get snow?

The Kashmir Stay AI

The most important feature I built for Kashmir Stay was the AI assistant. This was a deliberate strategic decision, not just a technical feature.

The problem with all travel discovery platforms — including traditional directories — is that they require the user to know what they are looking for and search for it correctly. But many tourists planning a Kashmir trip do not know the right terminology. They do not know that "Sonamarg" is a destination or that "shikara ride" is the Dal Lake experience they are imagining. They know what they want the experience to feel like — but not what to search for.

The Kashmir Stay AI solves this by letting tourists describe their trip in plain language — "I want 7 nights in Kashmir in September for a family of 4 with kids aged 8 and 12, mix of adventure and sightseeing, budget around ₹7,000 per night, need vegetarian food" — and instantly receiving matched listings from the verified business database that fit every parameter of that description.

This creates a fundamentally different and better experience than any OTA or traditional directory. It removes the skill of knowing how to search. It personalises the results. And because it only surfaces verified MBP listings, every recommendation it makes is a legitimate, quality-checked business.

How the Two Platforms Work Together

MakeMe Top and Kashmir Stay are separate platforms — separate domains, separate design systems, separate audiences — but they share one database and one purpose.

The flow:

  • Business owner lists on makemetop.in — gets Google-indexed within 48 hours, appears in Google search results
  • Same listing automatically appears on kashmirstay.in — visible to tourists browsing or using the AI assistant
  • Tourist finds the listing on Kashmir Stay or Google — contacts the business directly — direct booking, no commission

One listing fee. Presence on Google, on MBP, and on Kashmir Stay. For ₹799 per month.

What I Got Wrong — The Honest Version

Building two platforms has taught me more than any single project I have worked on. It has also produced mistakes I want to be honest about.

Mistake 1 — Trying to Launch Both Platforms Simultaneously

I initially tried to build and launch both platforms at the same time. This was a mistake. Building two full products in parallel means both get half your attention. MBP should have launched first, grown its listing database, and then Kashmir Stay should have launched with a populated database to offer tourists on day one.

Mistake 2 — Underestimating the Content Work

A directory is not just a technical product — it is a content product. The listing pages need descriptions, photos, categories, location data. Getting business owners to provide quality content for their listings requires significant education, guidance and sometimes hands-on help. I underestimated how much of the early work would be content-related rather than technical.

Mistake 3 — The First MBP Homepage Was Too Focused on Business Owners

The early MBP homepage was written entirely for business owners — pricing plans, badge verification, how to list. Tourists landing on it had no clear entry point and bounced. This taught me the two-platform lesson faster than anything else.

What These Platforms Mean for Kashmir

I want to be clear about what I am trying to build and what I am not.

I am not trying to compete with MakeMyTrip or Booking.com. Those are massive companies with hundreds of millions of dollars of infrastructure. I am not in that race.

What I am building is a locally-owned digital layer for Kashmir's tourism economy — a set of tools that gives J&K travel businesses direct access to tourists without the commission extraction that national OTAs represent.

If a family in Pahalgam that runs a resort gets 10 direct bookings per month through Kashmir Stay at ₹8,000 per booking — that is ₹80,000 staying entirely with their family. If those same 10 bookings came through a national OTA at 20% commission, ₹16,000 leaves J&K and goes to a corporate platform. At scale — across hundreds of businesses, thousands of bookings — the difference to Kashmir's local economy is significant.

That is what I am building toward.

What Is Next

Both platforms are continuing to grow and develop:

  • MakeMe Top (MBP): Expanding listing categories, improving business owner dashboard analytics, adding seasonal spotlight packages, growing the verified business database across all J&K districts
  • Kashmir Stay: Improving the AI assistant's recommendation quality, adding destination guides for under-discovered J&K locations, building the tourist-facing content layer, improving mobile UX for first-time visitors

Both platforms will always remain focused on J&K. I am not building a generic India-wide platform. The specificity is the point. A platform that knows Kashmir deeply will always serve Kashmir better than a platform that knows India broadly.

A Final Thought

I am from Srinagar. I grew up here. I have watched Kashmir's tourism economy navigate extraordinary challenges — political uncertainty, natural disasters, a global pandemic. Through all of it, the same businesses survived: the ones that had built genuine relationships with tourists, the ones that had a real product to offer, the ones that worked hard and delivered.

Those are the businesses MakeMe Top and Kashmir Stay exist to serve. Not the ones with the biggest marketing budgets. Not the ones who could afford OTA listings. The genuine ones — wherever they are in J&K — who deserve to be found.
